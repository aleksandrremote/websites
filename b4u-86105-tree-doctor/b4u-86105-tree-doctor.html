About Tree Doctor

Tree Doctor is Ben's crew for the tree jobs other companies avoid, the codominant oak leaning over your roof, the ash tree hollowed out by emerald ash borer, the dying pine wedged between the garage and the chicken house. Instead of jumping straight to removal, Ben looks at what's actually stable and what isn't, and if a tree can be saved with a cable rather than a chainsaw, that's what he'll recommend.

What Waterville keeps calling back for is the follow-through: showing up as scheduled even when it's nine degrees below zero, threading a cut between a house and a septic line without a scratch, and leaving a worksite so clean you'd think no one had been there at all. Wood gets cut and stacked so it's ready to split, brush gets hauled, and the price is one you can plan around instead of dread.

Common questions

Do you still work when it's brutally cold out here in Maine?

Yes. We've shown up and completed jobs at nine below zero without pushing back the schedule, and we're available 24/7 year-round.

My tree is splitting but not dead, does it have to come down?

Not necessarily. If the tree is stable and healthy, cabling the trunks together is often a cheaper, less invasive fix than removal.

What happens to the wood and debris after you take a tree down?

We cut limbs to firewood-ready lengths, stack them for you, and clear the site down to a few chips of sawdust before we leave.
